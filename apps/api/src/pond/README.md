# pond

The one pond, a person's home, the switch that opens matching, the public counter. TD-10, TD-13. #54, #581 (ADR-013): `GET /ponds/counter` needs no session and serves one number, the finished profiles, through `publishCounts` of `packages/schema` (nothing below k, with a floor of 10 under `matching_config.pond_counter_k`). `counter.ts` is the read. The counting is the nightly job `jobs/pond-counter.ts`, which reads across slices so that none depends on another for it, and moves the number only once at least k people have come or gone (`moveFigures`). `pond_counter` holds one row and no history (#607, ADR-013 §21).

#507 (ADR-013 §12 to §16, §18, §20): with a session of somebody with a home, the same route also says whom those people seek, as percentages of three bands of finished profiles in steps of five through `seeksShares` of `packages/schema`, or null until every band is at least k. That answer is `no-store` and the session is checked as on any other route; with no session the route says no band at all. The job counts the bands of people whose consent is of the current wording, which names them, and `moveFigures` moves them on their own.

#500, #607 (ADR-060): matching opens when an admin turns the one switch on, the `matching_config` key `matching_open` (`turnMatching` in `repo.ts`, under a lock of its own; `admin-routes.ts`: `GET /admin/matching`, `POST /admin/matching`, one `pond.matching` audit row a change, about the version it wrote). The tunables' screen neither lists nor writes the key. `GET /gate` tells the caller one of three things, incomplete, closed or open, handed its reader by the app: who is complete reads across slices and lives with the jobs (`jobs/pool.ts`). Who is in whose pool is `inEachOthersPool` of the matching slice.

#514 (ADR-061): the slice owns a person's one home. `places.ts` is pure over `places.generated.ts`, which `pnpm places` writes from Statistics Finland's list: `keptPlace` is what is kept of a postal code or a stored key, a place of at least `matching_config.home_min_inhabitants` people; `homeSummary` is what its owner reads; `shownPlace` is the name a card says. `home.ts` is the write and the reads. `PUT /account/home` takes five digits in its body, looks them up in memory and keeps the key, which is the postal code itself where the area is large enough and a municipality's number where it is not; an unknown code is refused by a constant. A person is in the one pond by having a home (§8, #607): no row stands for the pond, and `writeHomeOfAccount` writes the home alone. No log line, error, event, frame or push carries a home (§9).
